Property Tax

Section 13.1  |  Business Mathematics

The big idea

Cities, counties, and school districts pay for schools, roads, police, and fire protection with property tax. The owner of a building or piece of land pays this tax every year.

The tax is usually not figured on the full value of the property. It is figured on a smaller amount called the assessed value.

  • Fair market value is the price the property would sell for today.
  • Rate of assessment is the percent of the fair market value that gets taxed.
  • Assessed value (also called assessed valuation) is the part of the value that gets taxed.
  • Tax rate tells how much tax is charged on the assessed value.

1. Finding the assessed value

Assessed value = Fair market value × Rate of assessment

Always change the rate of assessment from a percent to a decimal before you multiply. To do that, divide by 100 (move the decimal point two places to the left).

Example
A property has a fair market value of $142,700 and a rate of assessment of 32%.
Change 32% to a decimal: 32 ÷ 100 = 0.32.
Multiply: $142,700 × 0.32 = $45,664. This is the assessed value.

2. Finding the tax rate a community needs

A community first decides how much money it needs. Then it divides that amount among all the assessed property in the area.

Tax rate = Total tax amount needed ÷ Total assessed value
Example
A community needs $19,100,000. The total assessed value is $7,150,000,000.
Divide: $19,100,000 ÷ $7,150,000,000 ≈ 0.002671.
Change to a percent by multiplying by 100: 0.002671 × 100 = 0.2671%.
Round to the nearest tenth of a percent: the hundredths digit is 6, so round up. The tax rate is 0.3%.

3. Four ways to write a tax rate

The same tax rate can be written four different ways. They all mean the same thing.

  • Percent means "per hundred." So 2.04% means $2.04 of tax for every $100 of assessed value. Percent and dollars per $100 always use the same number.
  • Dollars per $1000 is ten times the per $100 number, because $1000 is ten times $100.
  • Mills: one mill is $0.001 (one-tenth of a cent), so 1,000 mills make $1. That means mills and dollars per $1000 always use the same number.
Percent = per $100   → multiply by 10 →   per $1000 = mills
Example: starting with a percent
2.04% is the same as $2.04 per $100.
Multiply by 10: $20.40 per $1000.
Mills use the same number as per $1000: 20.4 mills.
Example: starting with dollars per $1000
$203.50 per $1000 is the same as 203.5 mills.
Divide by 10: $20.35 per $100.
Percent uses the same number as per $100: 20.35%.

4. Finding the tax

Tax = Assessed value × Tax rate (written as a decimal)

How you turn the tax rate into a decimal depends on how it is written:

  • A percent: divide by 100.   4.6% → 0.046
  • Dollars per $100: divide by 100.   $7.23 per $100 → 0.0723
  • Dollars per $1000: divide by 1000.   $75.91 per $1000 → 0.07591
  • Mills: divide by 1000.   44 mills → 0.044
Example: rate per $100
The assessed valuation is $88,000 and the tax rate is $7.23 per $100.
Decimal: 7.23 ÷ 100 = 0.0723.
Tax: $88,000 × 0.0723 = $6,362.40.
Example: rate in mills
The assessed valuation is $139,200 and the tax rate is 44 mills.
Decimal: 44 ÷ 1000 = 0.044.
Tax: $139,200 × 0.044 = $6,124.80.

5. Putting it all together

When a problem gives you the fair market value, work in two steps: find the assessed value first, then find the tax on the assessed value.

Example
A property has a fair market value of $328,300. The assessment rate is 26%, and the tax rate is 4.6%.
Step 1, assessed value: $328,300 × 0.26 = $85,358.
Step 2, tax: $85,358 × 0.046 = 3,926.468.
Round to the nearest cent: $3,926.47.

Watch out: never multiply the tax rate by the fair market value. The tax rate always goes with the assessed value.

6. Comparing two locations

To decide which place charges less tax, find the full tax in each place, then subtract.

Example
A building is worth $94,000. County A assesses at 40% with a rate of 49.3 mills. County B assesses at 12% with a rate of 53.0 mills.
County A: $94,000 × 0.40 = $37,600, then $37,600 × 0.0493 = $1,853.68.
County B: $94,000 × 0.12 = $11,280, then $11,280 × 0.053 = $597.84.
County B charges less. Savings: $1,853.68 − $597.84 = $1,255.84 per year.

Notice that County B has the higher mill rate but the lower tax, because its assessment rate is so much lower. Always work out the full tax before you decide.
